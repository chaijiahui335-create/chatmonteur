# Premium Talking-Head Director

Read this for a Chinese short-form business-owner/expert video when the user wants a more polished, visual, high-end result rather than a basic captioned talking head.

## Core directive

Do not edit from the transcript as if it were a subtitle file. Edit from the **meaning** of the transcript and turn meaning into a visual sequence.

The target is: real person first, clear story second, visual hierarchy third, decoration last.

## Required workflow

1. Transcribe the final audio spine with word timing.
2. Segment the speech into hook / problem / claim / explanation / example / proof / contrast / conclusion / CTA.
3. For every segment, choose one visual mode:
   - `speaker_clean`
   - `speaker_reframe`
   - `speaker_plus_text`
   - `speaker_plus_image`
   - `speaker_plus_video`
   - `speaker_cutout_plus_asset`
   - `full_asset`
   - `graphic_explainer`
4. Assign a reason to every non-base visual event.
5. Build one storyboard before rendering.
6. Render geometry and inserts from the storyboard.
7. Add captions as a separate top layer.
8. Add music/SFX only after picture decisions are locked.
9. Render at the source vertical resolution unless the user explicitly asks for another delivery size.
10. Run QC and inspect representative frames.

## Visual density

The video should not be visually flat, but it must breathe.

For a typical 20–60 second talking-head:

- Hook: 1–3 meaningful visual events.
- Main body: roughly one meaningful visual change every 2–6 seconds, adjusted to speech rhythm.
- High-information moments may use faster changes.
- Emotional or credibility moments may intentionally hold longer.
- Never create a change solely because a timer expired.

A visual event can be a crop change, text composition, product insert, screenshot, B-roll clip, graphic, or cutout composition. It does not have to be a transition.

## Text is a visual layer

For important statements, do not simply enlarge the transcript. Extract a short phrase that carries the idea.

Use three levels:

- caption: what is being said
- emphasis: the key phrase the viewer should remember
- title/card: the structural idea of the section

Prefer 2–8 Chinese characters for emphasis when possible. Preserve the speaker's exact meaning; do not invent claims.

## Image and video inserts

Use assets to answer a visual question:

- What object is being discussed?
- What example is being described?
- What evidence is being referenced?
- What process would be clearer if shown?
- What comparison needs a visual?

Prefer, in order:

1. user-provided real footage
2. user-provided product photos/screenshots/documents
3. project-owned graphics
4. licensed/approved stock
5. generated illustration only when the idea cannot be shown otherwise

Do not use generic stock simply because it looks attractive.

## Product/business-owner footage

When the speaker is holding or demonstrating a product, treat the product as a primary asset. Use:

- close crop of the product
- detail crop
- speaker + product side-by-side
- speaker cutout + product image
- short product B-roll

Do not replace a useful live demonstration with unrelated stock.

## Premium composition rules

- Preserve a clear focal point.
- Use negative space for emphasis text.
- Keep text and assets off the face and mouth.
- Use a consistent visual grammar across the whole video.
- Prefer 2–3 strong layout families over 10 unrelated templates.
- Use motion to guide attention, not to announce every cut.
- Avoid excessive rounded cards, emoji, stickers, bounce animations, and fake "AI" effects unless the brand specifically calls for them.

## Asset transitions

When moving from speaker to an asset, prefer a motivated transition:

- hard cut on a semantic boundary
- short push/reframe into the asset
- masked reveal when the speaker points to or introduces the asset
- picture-in-picture when the speaker remains useful

Return to the speaker when the asset's informational job is complete.

## Advanced masking

Cut out the speaker only for selected explanation moments. Keep the cutout large enough to read facial expression and small enough to leave clean space for the asset or text.

Never use a cutout for the entire runtime just because segmentation is available.

## Anti-AI-look rules

Reject the plan when:

- every sentence has a zoom
- every sentence has a new picture
- captions, emphasis text and title text appear simultaneously without hierarchy
- stock footage is used as filler
- transitions are louder than the content
- the same composition repeats mechanically
- the speaker disappears for too long
- the video contains more decoration than information

The desired impression is **intentional editorial design**, not maximum effect count.

## Delivery

Default vertical master: preserve the source vertical resolution and frame rate where practical. Do not create a low-resolution preview and call it the final. If a proxy is made for review, label it explicitly as `PREVIEW` and render the delivery master separately.
