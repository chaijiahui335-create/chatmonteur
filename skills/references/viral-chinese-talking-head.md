# Viral Chinese Talking-Head Style Guide

This reference is based on recurring patterns in current Chinese short-form talking-head editing tutorials and high-retention examples: strong opening hook, deliberate rhythm, selective 花字, semantic supplementary visuals, keyframe-based framing, restrained sound design, and clean composition. It is a style reference, not a template to copy literally.

## 1. Hook first

The first 1-3 seconds must establish the subject, tension, curiosity, or a concrete visual object. Use the strongest usable spoken line; if the source has no strong opening, reorder only when the meaning remains truthful.

Possible hook treatments:
- tight crop on speaker
- immediate product/detail insert
- large 2-8 character headline derived from the spoken idea
- speaker + oversized keyword composition
- one decisive sound cue

Never begin with a logo animation or decorative title unless the brand requirement demands it.

## 2. Edit speech for rhythm, not speed

Remove dead air, false starts, repeats, and unusable takes. Keep meaningful pauses. Do not make the speaker sound breathless.

Use shot changes at semantic boundaries rather than fixed every-N-seconds cuts. A visual change is especially valuable after a complete thought, a contrast, a number, an example, or a conclusion.

## 3. Three text layers

1. Captions: compact, readable support for what is being said.
2. 花字 / emphasis: short phrase extracted from the spoken idea; use selectively.
3. Headline card: only for hook, section turn, key conclusion, or strong contrast.

Do not turn every sentence into a giant caption.

## 4. Supplementary visuals are evidence

For product/business talking-head content, prefer:
- real product close-ups
- details of the item being discussed
- store/scene footage
- screenshots/documents when relevant
- before/after or comparison visuals
- simple diagrams or numbers

The visual must have a stated reason tied to the spoken anchor. Generic stock footage is a last resort.

## 5. Visual grammar

Use a small set of recurring compositions so the video feels designed rather than chaotic:

- A: clean full-frame speaker
- B: tight speaker punch-in
- C: speaker + product/detail card
- D: full-screen product/detail insert
- E: speaker cutout + supporting visual
- F: contrast/number card
- G: clean return to speaker

A typical 20-30 second clip should move through several of these states, but not mechanically.

## 6. Motion

Use keyframes for purposeful push-ins, reframes, and exits. Avoid constant zooms, shakes, spins, and preset transitions. Motion should reinforce the spoken beat.

## 7. Sound

Voice remains dominant. Use short, motivated SFX for text/visual entrances and important beats. Music should establish energy without competing with speech. Avoid adding a sound to every cut.

## 8. Ending

The last 1-2 seconds should resolve visually: return to the speaker, show the product, land a conclusion, or provide a clean CTA. Avoid abrupt unfinished tails.

## 9. Anti-template checks

Reject if:
- every sentence has identical subtitle treatment
- every 2 seconds has the same zoom
- every visual insert is stock footage
- the screen is filled with text
- decorative effects outnumber informative visuals
- the edit could be described as 'auto captions plus effects'

## 10. Reference principle

The goal is to reproduce the *editing logic* seen in strong Chinese short-form content, not to clone any creator's exact typography, music, or transition package.
