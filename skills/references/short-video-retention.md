# Short-Video Retention Rules

## Opening

The first seconds should establish a clear reason to continue. Prefer the strongest usable spoken idea rather than a generic intro or logo animation.

## Information rhythm

Alternate between:

- speaker credibility
- concrete visual proof
- concise emphasis text
- return to speaker

Do not create constant stimulation. Contrast is more valuable than speed.

## Repetition control

If two neighboring segments use the same layout, caption treatment, motion, and asset class, redesign one unless the repetition is intentional.

## Payoff

The conclusion should visually resolve the promise of the opening. A strong final phrase may receive a clean emphasis card or tighter speaker frame, but avoid a generic end screen unless the project requires it.

## CTA

Keep calls to action short and visually subordinate to the actual content. Never let CTA graphics obscure the speaker or become a second competing headline.
