# Premium Text Layout — Chinese Short Video

## Principle

Text must create hierarchy, not duplicate speech. The viewer should immediately know what is ordinary narration and what deserves attention.

## Three levels

### Level 1 — Spoken captions

Short phrase groups synchronized to speech. They provide accessibility and comprehension.

### Level 2 — Editorial emphasis

A short phrase, normally 2–8 Chinese characters, extracted from the spoken idea. Use for the core claim, contrast, number, or conclusion. It may be larger than captions and may appear independently.

### Level 3 — Title / statement card

A concise statement used only at major structural moments such as the opening hook, a section change, or final conclusion.

## Composition rules

- Never show the full transcript as the main graphic.
- Avoid placing all text at the bottom if it creates a monotonous template.
- Use safe zones appropriate to 9:16 mobile viewing.
- Preserve the speaker's face and hands.
- Keep one dominant text message per shot.
- Use scale, position, weight, and timing to establish hierarchy before adding decoration.
- Use emphasis sparingly; if every word is emphasized, nothing is emphasized.

## Timing

Text should enter close to the semantic anchor phrase and leave when its informational job is complete. Do not keep a large emphasis phrase on screen for an entire paragraph.

## Visual grammar

Prefer combinations such as:

- small caption + one large keyword
- short statement + supporting product image
- number + tiny explanatory caption
- two-line contrast: `不是 A` / `而是 B`
- keyword near the relevant object or speaker gesture

## Anti-patterns

- giant subtitles covering the torso
- five colors in one sentence
- every word bouncing independently
- decorative English text with no meaning
- permanent title bars
- text effects that compete with a product or face
