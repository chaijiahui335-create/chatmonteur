# Visual Asset System for Chinese Video Accounts

## Goal

Make the edit visually rich without turning it into a stock-footage montage. Visual assets must explain, prove, contextualize, or deliberately reset attention.

## Asset types

1. User-provided product footage
2. User-provided photos and screenshots
3. Relevant short video inserts
4. Simple diagrams, numbers, arrows, callouts, and comparison cards
5. Approved external visuals when a source is available and rights are appropriate

## Decision hierarchy

Use the strongest real asset available. Prefer a real product/store/customer/process visual over generic stock imagery.

## Insert patterns

### Speaker + asset

Keep the speaker visible while a relevant image/video appears in a side panel, card, or masked layer.

### Full-screen insert

Use when the asset itself is the clearest way to show the point. Keep it short and return to the speaker after the idea lands.

### Picture-in-picture

Use for screenshots, interfaces, examples, or evidence that benefits from the speaker's commentary remaining visible.

### Cutout composition

Use a speaker cutout with a simple background or relevant visual for selected emphasis moments.

## Asset selection test

For every inserted visual record:

- semantic anchor phrase
- reason for insertion
- source asset
- intended duration
- composition mode

Reject assets that are merely aesthetically pleasing but semantically empty.

## Motion of inserted assets

Prefer subtle scale, position, or crop movement over flashy transitions. Do not animate every asset independently.

## External assets

Do not silently scrape or use copyrighted media when a permitted source is unavailable. When external assets are needed, make the source/rights decision explicit in the edit plan.
