# Semantic Asset Matching

## Goal

Choose images and video inserts because they clarify the spoken idea, not because a visual slot is available.

## Asset categories

- product/detail footage
- real business footage
- screenshots/interfaces
- photos/documents
- diagrams/numbers
- contextual B-roll
- simple generated graphics

## Matching process

For each candidate insert:

1. Extract the spoken anchor phrase.
2. Convert it to a concrete visual concept.
3. Search available project assets first.
4. Rank candidates by semantic relevance, visual clarity, authenticity, and continuity.
5. Reject candidates that are generic, ambiguous, misleading, or repetitive.

## Authenticity preference

Prefer the user's real product, store, staff, screenshots, customer-facing materials, and original footage over generic stock whenever possible. Real material makes an owner/expert video feel credible and specific.

## Duration

The insert should last only long enough to establish or explain the idea. Use a shorter insert for a visual accent and a longer insert only when the viewer needs to inspect a process or detail.

## Repetition control

Do not reuse the same asset repeatedly in one short video unless repetition is itself part of the argument.

## Missing asset

If a useful visual is required but no suitable asset exists, prefer a clean graphic, crop, zoom, or speaker+text composition over an unrelated stock clip.
