# Chinese Short-Form Text Layout System

## Principle

Do not treat speech recognition output as the finished subtitle design. Build a visual text system from semantic phrases.

## Three text levels

### 1. Spoken captions

Small, stable phrase-level captions that preserve what the speaker says. They should be easy to follow without dominating the frame.

### 2. Emphasis text

Short 2-8 character phrases extracted from the current idea. Use these for the main point, contrast, number, warning, or conclusion. Do not repeat every spoken word.

### 3. Headline cards

Use only at major structural turns: hook, section transition, or final takeaway. Keep them short enough to read immediately.

## Layout modes

Choose based on the composition instead of rotating through templates:

- lower-third phrase: default for uninterrupted talking head
- upper/lower split: when the lower frame is occupied
- side text + speaker: when a screenshot or product visual shares the frame
- center punch text: only for a very short high-value phrase
- full-frame title card: only for a structural transition

## Typography rules

- One primary font family per project.
- Use weight, size, and position to create hierarchy before adding color.
- Emphasis should be selective; do not highlight complete sentences by default.
- Keep text away from eyes, mouth, hands holding the product, and important visual evidence.
- Keep safe margins for 9:16 platforms.
- Avoid dense paragraphs and multi-line blocks that require the viewer to stop reading.

## Timing rules

- Phrase captions follow the speaker's natural semantic rhythm.
- Emphasis text enters on the anchor phrase and exits after the idea lands.
- Do not animate every word.
- Avoid more than one major text event at the same instant unless the composition explicitly calls for it.

## Editorial test

For every emphasis phrase, ask: "If the viewer only noticed these few words, would they understand what the speaker is emphasizing?" If not, rewrite the emphasis phrase rather than enlarging more text.
